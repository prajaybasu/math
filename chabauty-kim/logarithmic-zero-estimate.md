# A zero estimate for triangular logarithmic systems

Let $k=\mathbb F_p$, let $S\subset k$ be a nonempty finite pole set and fix a jet centre $b\in k$. In a common ordered basis of size $N$, take constant strictly upper triangular matrices $A_a$ for $a\in S$. Suppose their common **left** kernel is the line generated by the coordinate row $e$. Set
$$
T=\prod_{a\in S\cup\{b\}}(X-a),\qquad d=\deg T-1.
$$
Use a polynomial column $w$ with $e w=1$ and satisfying the jet equation
$$
T w'\equiv \sum_{a\in S}\frac{T}{X-a} A_a w\pmod{(X-b)^p}.
$$
At an ordinary centre one can start with any column value satisfying $ew(b)=1$ and solve the unscaled equation coefficient by coefficient through degree $p-1$. The recursion divides only by $1,\ldots,p-1$. The factor $X-b$ in $T$ absorbs the last derivative defect. At a singular centre the jet equation is a hypothesis.

**Zero estimate.** For a nonzero polynomial row $P$ of degree at most $A$, put $H=NA+d\binom N2$. If $H<p$, then $P w$ has a nonzero coefficient of order at most $H$ at $b$.

## Proof

On rows set $D h=T h'+\sum_a (T/(X-a))h A_a$. Then $D^jP$ has degree at most $A+jd$. On substitution, $D$ acts as $T\,d/dX$ modulo $(X-b)^p$. This operator preserves every ideal $(X-b)^m$, as $T(b)=0$.

Let $M$ consist of the first $r$ independent successive iterates, stopping immediately before the first dependence. Their rational span is stable under $D$ by the Leibniz rule. Dividing by $T$ gives stability under $h\mapsto h'+\sum_a hA_a/(X-a)$. By the last-pivot-row construction, the span contains a normalized horizontal row $h$. Each coordinate is a ratio of minors of degree at most $H_r=rA+d\binom r2\le H$. [Last-pivot-row argument](zero-estimate.md#2-a-stable-row-space-and-its-smallest-leading-monomial)

For each column $j$, horizontality expresses $h_j'$ as a sum of simple fractions whose coefficients involve only $h_i$ with $i<j$. Inductively those earlier coordinates are constant. A rational derivative has zero residue at every pole, so every coefficient in this partial fraction expansion vanishes. Now $h_j'=0$. A reduced fraction with numerator and denominator of degree less than $p$ and zero derivative is constant. Induction gives $h\in k^N$ and $h A_a=0$ at every pole. The kernel hypothesis and pivot normalization give $h=e$.

Clearing the pivot minor gives $q e$ in the polynomial span of the rows of $M$, with $0\ne q\in k[X]$ of degree at most $H_r$. If $Pw$ vanished modulo $(X-b)^{H_r+1}$, so would every row evaluation and hence $q e w=q$. This contradicts the degree of $q$.

## Iterated-integral words

On a finite set of distinct words closed under deleting the first letter, include the empty word and order by decreasing length. For each letter $a$ take the constant matrix sending the row $e_{av}$ to $e_v$ and every row with a different first letter to zero. The common left kernel is exactly the empty-word line, since the $v$ coordinate of the image under $a$ recovers the coefficient of $av$. Nonzero fixed scalar factors on the letter matrices do not change the kernel. The estimate therefore applies to polynomial-coefficient linear forms in these word jets whenever the jet equation holds.

For the strict one-variable finite multiple polylogarithms take
$$
F_{s_1,\ldots,s_h}(X)=\sum_{p>n_1>\cdots>n_h\ge1}
\frac{X^{n_1}}{n_1^{s_1}\cdots n_h^{s_h}},\qquad F_{\varnothing}=1.
$$
Use the word $0^{s_1-1}1\cdots 0^{s_h-1}1$ and $b=0$. For $s_1>1$, $XF'_{s_1,\mathbf t}=F_{s_1-1,\mathbf t}$. For $s_1=1$, summing the geometric progression above the first tail index gives
$$
X(1-X)F'_{1,\mathbf t}=X F_{\mathbf t}(X)-X^pF_{\mathbf t}(1).
$$
These identities give the required jets at $0$ with pole set $\{0,1\}$. Multiplying the cleared equation by a nonzero scalar to use $X(1-X)$ has no effect. Applications to polynomial expressions require a nonzero word expansion. For example $F_1^2-2F_{1,1}$ vanishes modulo $X^p$ for odd $p$, by comparing coefficients below $p$ using $1/(i(n-i))=(1/i+1/(n-i))/n$. Relations for several conventions of finite multiple polylogarithms occur in [Ono–Yamamoto](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06693) and [Sakugawa–Seki](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07653).

## A quadratic lower bound at two fixed poles

Fix $n\ge2$. Over $\mathbb Q[[X]]$, define the operators
$$
J_u f=\int_0^X\frac{f(t)}{1-t}\,dt,\qquad
J_v f=\int_0^X\frac{f(t)}{1+t}\,dt .
$$
Map an $n$-by-$n$ array $(b_{ij})$ to the first $n^2-1$ Taylor coefficients, starting with the constant term, of
$$
G=\sum_{0\le i,j<n} b_{ij}J_u^i J_v J_u^j1.
$$
This is a rational linear map to a space of dimension $n^2-1$. Pick a nonzero kernel element and clear denominators. Let $k$ be its last nonzero row index. Realize $G$ in a system with coordinates $U_0,\ldots,U_k,V_0,\ldots,V_{n-1}$ by setting
$$
V_{n-1}=1,\quad V_j=J_u V_{j+1}\ (j<n-1),\qquad
U_i=J_u U_{i+1}+J_v\sum_j b_{ij} V_{n-1-j},\quad U_{k+1}=0.
$$
Expansion gives $U_0=G$. Differentiation gives a triangular constant-residue system of rank $R=k+1+n\le2n$ with poles $1,-1$. The $u$ residue shifts both chains. Its left kernel is spanned by the two terminal rows. The $v$ residue takes the terminal $U$ row to the nonzero last row of the array in reverse order and kills the terminal $V$ row. The joint kernel is exactly the line of $V_{n-1}=1$.

For every sufficiently large prime, the last array row stays nonzero. Reduce the jets through degree $p-1$ at the ordinary centre $0$, where the Taylor denominators through that degree are units. The constant row selecting $U_0$ still vanishes to order at least $n^2-1$. It is genuinely nonzero in the truncated ring by the zero estimate above, taking $p>2\binom R2$ as well as $p>n^2$. Thus the order can reach at least $R^2/4-1$ in unbounded rank, even with constant input and these same two poles. There is no uniform bound of order $R(A+1)$ for this class. A sharper estimate for the symmetric powers of the classical polylogarithm system would need to use their additional structure.

## Formal scope

Lean proves the finite-pole residue lemma, the horizontal-row classification, and the matrix nonvanishing theorem for an independent set of polynomial rows with stable rational span. It also proves the dimension lemma for the Taylor map and the two-chain joint-kernel calculation. The cyclic-iterate specialization, the word jet identities and the realization of the lower-bound example are proved above. A Chabauty–Kim application on another curve additionally requires an equation for its locus, its reduction to these jets and a count of candidate residues.
